Round 3 · tabs instead of folding a group is a box at the root and a tab of its own

No fold flag, no convexity rule, no group region. The root tab shows every ungrouped pass as a node and every group as one box whose ports are its boundary edges: a member sampler that reads an outside pass (or nothing) is an input port, a member read from outside (plus the bundle's own output) is an output port. The box's picture is the bundle output's live texture. A group's tab shows its members as ordinary nodes and the outside passes they touch as dimmed ghosts at the border. Depth is two by 091's no-nesting rule, so the tab row is also the breadcrumb.

A · the root tab, one port per sampler slot every wire is a real sampler

Both bloom members that read scene get their own port, named member.sampler because the sampler name alone is not unique. Honest, and three wires from the same source into one box is what it looks like.

B · the root tab, ports merged by what feeds them the black-box interface

Slots fed by the same outside pass fold into one port that says how many slots it carries; unfilled slots fold by sampler name. A wire dropped on a merged port rewrites every slot behind it. The box now reads as a filter with one scene input, which is what a user who never opens it wants.

C · the bloom tab members as nodes, the outside as ghosts

What you see after clicking the tab or double-clicking the box. scene and final are ghosts: dimmed, dashed, not editable, ports kept so a wire can still be dragged across the boundary; clicking one jumps back to the root with that node selected.

D · a non-convex group the case folding could not carry

A hand-made document: the host's grade reads fx_bright and feeds fx_blur, so a path between two members leaves the group. At the root the box has an output wired into grade and an input wired back from it, drawn as a back edge on the bus; in the group tab grade is a ghost on both sides. Nothing is refused.

E · a generator box Radiance Cascades imported whole, no inputs

The cascades' entry point paint has no sampler at all, so imported with paint kept the box has an output and nothing else: a source you drop into a chain. Imported with paint substituted by the host's own paint, the three members that read it fold into one merged input port carrying three slots.

F · free placement, a wire being dragged, the menus positions are the pass's; arrange is a verb

Every node sits where it was put (the first sight of a document runs the rank layout once and stores it). A freshly added pass lands under the cursor, unconnected, dashed until it compiles. A wire is being dragged from scene's output dot toward the new pass's u_src port; the drop writes that sampler's source. The dotted guides are the snap during drag: the node aligns to a neighbour's column or row. The two menus are the node's and the canvas's.

Round 2 · port-list nodes the chosen shape, six ways

A node lists its samplers as ports; an edge ends at the port's dot and carries no label. What varies: where the list sits on the node, which way the ranks run, and whether the lanes shape from round 1 is folded in. The output port is the dot on the right of the picture in every variant.

C · name as a header bar the node-editor look; the group tints the bar

The name moves to a bar on top of the node, the picture under it, the ports under that. The output pass gets an accent-tinted bar. With the region toggle off, the bar's tint alone says which group a node is in.

D · ports beside the picture the node stays the tile's height

The port column sits left of the picture, so a node with three samplers is no taller than one with none; the node is wider instead. Edges arrive at the same height band as the picture, which keeps the rows straight.

E · ranks top to bottom, ports on the left edges drop, then turn into the port

The chain runs down. Every edge leaves the source's bottom, drops to the gap under it, runs to the left of the consumer and turns into the port dot; a long edge does the same, further left. Nothing needs a label and nothing bows.

F · lanes with port-list nodes round 1's #4 and #5 together

One column in strip order, every edge a lane on the left ending in the port dot. The narrowest picture of all, and the only one where the ports and the lanes are the same idea: a lane is a wire, a port is where it lands.
